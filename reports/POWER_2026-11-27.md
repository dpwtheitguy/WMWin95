# Power applet research — 27 November 2026

Research only; no Power applet was registered, no power policy installed and no
host/session power operation was performed. The parent redirected this secondary
stream to improve the existing Network prototype while Themes remains primary.

Original private `powercfg.dll` identifies itself as Advanced Power Management
Control Panel Applet4.00.1111, exporting `CPlApplet`. Its original dialog resources
cover APM enable/status and battery display, Advanced/Troubleshooting options,
disk spin-down and PC-Card modem power. `apm.inf` installs it with POWER.DRV and
VPOWERD; POWERCFG.CPL is listed as obsolete in `winver.inf`.

Monitor DPMS timeout controls are not the native Power sheet. Those belong with
Display/Screen Saver. Mapping a monitor-only DPMS toggle onto the Power sheet's
system-wide APM-enable checkbox would falsely imply a larger effect. Do not
introduce such a control merely to claim the next applet functional.

The classic reference did not surface a Power sheet after the normal control
commands in this session. This observation alone does not prove a particular APM
BIOS/driver state. No setting was forced and no OEM driver installed. Source
resources are available for further HWND/native fixture measurement; no new
Power pixel-acceptance claim is made. `before-power-applet-20261127` was verified,
restored and the classic VM checked running afterward; no floppy was attached.
Raw diagnostic captures remain under `private/power-20261127/`.

Future implementation needs actual UPower/power-profiles/logind capabilities,
read-only real battery/mains status, specific hardware absence reasons, a scoped
normal-account backend, Apply/Cancel/readback and rollback. Disk spin-down must
remain device-specific and opt-in; do not send hdparm/suspend/shutdown commands
as an automated host test. Add monitor DPMS timers to the historical Display
location with an owned-Xvfb effect test. Linux equivalents and old APM flags are
not necessarily one-to-one; document each mapping and missing effect.

Primary historical context: Microsoft's archived [Enabling APM Support After
Windows95 Setup](https://jeffpar.github.io/kbarchive/kb/135/Q135136/), plus the
local original `apm.inf`, `winver.inf` and POWERCFG resources. No third-party
screenshot is used as independent native acceptance evidence.
